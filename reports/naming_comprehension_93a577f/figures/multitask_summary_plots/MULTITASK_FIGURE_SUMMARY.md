# Phase 3 — interleaved multitask figures

Generated by `scripts/naming_comprehension/make_multitask_summary_plots.py` from
existing Phase 3A/3B/3C outputs only. No training, no new analysis, no
scientific artifact modified.

## Warm-start caveat

All Phase 3 experiments start from the canonical repetition-trained seed22/e140
checkpoint. They therefore test the acquisition of naming and comprehension by
an already mature repetition system. Joint development from random
initialization is a separate, untested question.

## Shared setup

Schedule M2 = repetition : naming : comprehension = 1 : 2 : 3, interleaved at
the optimizer-step level, one shared AdamW (lr 1e-4, weight decay 1e-5, batch
64), union LTM scope, comprehension retrieval against all 29,571 GloVe vectors.
Naming and comprehension always operate on the validated subset3288.

## Fig 1 — Interleaved training enables local three-task coexistence
`fig1_multitask_local_coexistence.png` / `.pdf`

- **Question** — can naming and comprehension be added to a mature repetition
  system without destroying it, on the population all three tasks share?
- **Training regime** — Phase 3A M2 continued exactly into Phase 3B; one
  continuous 0 → 780k trajectory. Repetition rehearsed on subset3288.
- **Population** — subset3288 for all three tasks.
- **Metric** — repetition LTM exact, naming exact, comprehension top-1 (%),
  95% criterion line.
- **Main observation** — naming reaches 100% by ~60k and repetition LTM returns
  to ceiling early; comprehension climbs slowly and is the binding constraint.
  All four conditions are first met simultaneously at 720k, and the criterion is
  confirmed at 760k + 780k (R 100% / N 100% / C 95.1%).
- **Supported interpretation** — when repetition is rehearsed on the same
  population as naming and comprehension, all three can eventually coexist in
  the same LTM parameters.
- **Important limitation** — coexistence is not monotonically stable. Repetition
  LTM shows transient dips well below 95% during training (visible in the curve),
  and comprehension itself dipped back under threshold at 740k between two
  successful snapshots.

## Fig 2 — Rehearsal population determines the preservation–acquisition trade-off
`fig2_local_vs_global_rehearsal.png` / `.pdf`

- **Question** — what does the choice of repetition rehearsal population change?
- **Training regime** — Phase 3B (local) vs Phase 3C (full-lexicon), both M2
  1:2:3, both from canonical seed22/e140. Phase 3C is an independent run, not a
  continuation of 3B.
- **Population** — the main experimental difference is the repetition
  population: **Phase 3B R_3288** vs **Phase 3C R_29571**. Naming and
  comprehension remain on the same subset3288 and the M2 1:2:3 schedule is
  unchanged.
- **Metric** — four endpoint values in %: comprehension top-1, naming exact,
  repetition LTM on subset3288, repetition LTM on the full lexicon.
- **Main observation** — 3B: 95.1 / 100.0 / 100.0 / **11.8**. 3C: 91.5 / 100.0 /
  97.3 / **97.1**.
- **Supported interpretation** — local rehearsal allows stronger specialization
  on the 3288 population but forgets historical mappings outside it;
  full-lexicon rehearsal preserves historical repetition globally, while
  comprehension acquisition is slower.
- **Important limitation** — because the repetition population is ~9× larger
  while repetition task-step frequency is fixed, the number of repetition
  presentations per item is also much lower in Phase 3C (≈288 passes vs 2500).
  The comparison therefore changes BOTH the breadth/coverage of rehearsal AND
  the repetition exposure per individual item. These two effects are **not
  separable** from Phase 3B vs Phase 3C alone. Endpoints also differ slightly
  (780k vs 800k).

## Fig 3 — Full-lexicon rehearsal preserves historical repetition
`fig3_global_repetition_preservation.png` / `.pdf`

- **Question** — how much of the historical repetition system survives?
- **Training regime** — canonical seed22/e140 vs Phase 3B @780k vs Phase 3C
  @800k.
- **Population** — the full canonical 29,571-word lexicon, canonical
  forced-length AR repetition evaluator.
- **Metric** — repetition exact-match (%) per route: FULL, WM, LTM.
- **Main observation** — WM is numerically unchanged in all three conditions
  (0.999763). Local-only rehearsal collapses the ventral route to 11.8% and
  drags FULL down to 83.2%. Full-lexicon rehearsal keeps LTM at 97.1% and FULL
  at ceiling (100.0%).
- **Supported interpretation** — rehearsing the whole lexicon is what protects
  the historical ventral mapping; the dorsal route was never at risk since it is
  frozen.
- **Important limitation** — LTM is **not** perfectly preserved: 97.1% is 1.8
  absolute points below the canonical 98.9%.

## Fig backup — Repetition is initially destabilized before recovering
`fig_backup_ltm_recovery_dynamics.png` / `.pdf`

- **Question** — is the historical solution simply held in place, or perturbed?
- **Training regime** — early-to-mid training (0 → 200k), 3B vs 3C.
- **Population** — subset3288 LTM repetition; plus the fixed out-of-subset probe
  for Phase 3C (dashed).
- **Metric** — repetition exact-match (%).
- **Main observation** — both runs dip sharply around 5k (3B ≈ 77.9%,
  3C ≈ 60.7%) and then recover; 3B returns to ceiling by ~20k, 3C climbs back
  above 95% by ~100k. The 3C probe tracks the subset curve closely.
- **Supported interpretation** — the historical LTM solution is not frozen in
  place; training initially destabilizes it, followed by recovery under
  repetition rehearsal.
- **Important limitation** — the out-of-subset probe was introduced with the
  Phase 3C implementation, so **no probe exists for Phase 3B**; only the 3C
  probe can be shown. Backup figure, not part of the main three.
