# Naming & Comprehension — summary figures

Generated by `scripts/naming_comprehension/make_summary_plots.py` from existing
run outputs only. No training was launched; no scientific output was modified.

In every run below, one epoch = one complete pass over the training population,
so **epochs and exposures per item are the same quantity**. Axes use exposures
per item because that is what is comparable across population sizes.

Main package: Fig 1 → Fig 5. Fig backup is supporting context.

---

## Fig 1 — Naming acquisition slows sharply with lexical scale
`fig1_naming_scaling_curve.png` / `.pdf`

- **Question** — how does naming acquisition cost change with the number of
  mappings trained simultaneously?
- **Population** — representative N=3,288 / N=10,000 / full lexicon N=29,571.
- **Metric** — free-AR exact-match (%), criterion line at 95%.
- **Comparison** — three population sizes at matched exposures per item.
- **Main interpretation** — naming is learnable, but acquisition cost rises
  steeply with N: criterion at 550 exposures (N=3,288), 1,200 (N=10,000), and
  9.8% after 3,000 exposures at N=29,571. The full-lexicon curve is still rising
  but decelerating; this is a cost result, not a proof of impossibility.
- **Decision enabled** — flat single-task training over the whole lexicon is not
  a practical route, and the cost curve is not extrapolable from two points.

## Fig 2 — The same mappings are learned in ~10k blocks but not jointly at full scale
`fig2_same_words_alone_vs_full.png` / `.pdf`

- **Question** — is the full-lexicon result caused by intrinsically hard words,
  or by simultaneous-population scale?
- **Population** — three **disjoint** representative blocks whose union is exactly
  the 29,571 naming items (A=[0:10k), B=[10k:20k), C=[20k:29,571)).
- **Metric** — free-AR exact-match (%).
- **Comparison** — each block trained alone vs the *same words* measured inside
  the full-lexicon endpoint.
- **Main interpretation** — every part of the lexicon is learned at ~10k scale
  (97.5 / 97.2 / 98.4%), while the identical words sit at 9.6 / 9.6 / 10.1% when
  all 29,571 are trained together. Intrinsic item difficulty does not explain the
  full-lexicon failure, and neither does composition (separately controlled at
  fixed N).
- **Decision enabled** — the effect depends on how many mappings are trained at
  once, not on which mappings they are.

## Fig 3 — Comprehension is learnable, but retrieval converges slowly
`fig3_comprehension_learning_curve.png` / `.pdf`

- **Question** — is comprehension learnable in the current architecture, and at
  what cost?
- **Population** — band-stratified subset3288, objective C3, retrieval against
  the **full 29,571-word GloVe bank** (never shrunk to the subset).
- **Metric** — top-1 retrieval (%), criterion line at 95%.
- **Comparison** — single final run; earlier truncated budgets are superseded.
- **Main interpretation** — comprehension reaches the criterion (confirmed at
  3,850 exposures = 200,200 optimizer steps) with **no** architecture, scope,
  objective or hyper-parameter change. Top-1 first touches 95% at 3,700, dips
  below at 3,750, then holds at 3,800 and 3,850; only the confirmation epoch is
  annotated because the criterion requires two consecutive evaluations. The long
  approach shows the apparent plateau at 1,000 exposures (85.4%) was a budget
  artefact, not a ceiling.
- **Decision enabled** — the current architecture can learn the comprehension
  mapping at this scale; the main observed difficulty is slow retrieval
  optimization in dense GloVe neighbourhoods.

## Fig 4 — Single-task training preserves WM but collapses LTM repetition
`fig4_repetition_cost_single_task.png` / `.pdf`

- **Question** — what does single-task training do to repetition?
- **Population** — matched full-scale single-task runs (100 epochs each),
  canonical forced-length AR repetition evaluator over all 29,571 words.
- **Metric** — repetition exact-match (%) per route (FULL / WM / LTM).
- **Comparison** — frozen model vs after comprehension vs after naming, same
  evaluator and same budget so the two tasks are directly comparable.
- **Main interpretation** — WM (dorsal) is numerically unchanged (0.999763 in
  every condition, bit-identical), FULL barely moves, while LTM (ventral) falls
  from 98.9% to 1.4% (comprehension) and 0.14% (naming). The damage is specific
  to the ventral route. *Complementary information, deliberately not in the bar
  plot to keep the comparison matched:* with a much larger naming budget the LTM
  route degrades further still, to ≈0.03% after 3,000 exposures (Phase 2H).
- **Decision enabled** — motivates training that actively preserves the ventral
  repetition route rather than single-task fine-tuning.

## Fig 5 — Sequential naming causes catastrophic forgetting and slows new learning
`fig5_sequential_forgetting.png` / `.pdf`

- **Question** — what happens when a model that has learned Block A is then
  trained only on Block B?
- **Population** — Block A (10,000 items, evaluated only, **zero gradient**) and
  Block B (10,000 items, trained), disjoint; plus Block B trained from the
  canonical checkpoint as an acquisition control.
- **Metric** — free-AR exact-match (%) vs Block-B exposures per item.
- **Comparison** — A retention and B-after-A from the sequential run, against
  B-alone (dashed control).
- **Main interpretation** — naive sequential staging fails in both directions:
  Block A is almost completely forgotten within the first 50 Block-B exposures
  (97.51% → 0.02%, then ≈0%), while Block B itself is much slower to acquire from
  the A-specialized checkpoint than from canonical initialization (34.5% at 1,500
  exposures vs ~97% by 1,300 alone). These are two distinct phenomena —
  catastrophic forgetting and path dependence — and neither is reducible to a
  capacity limitation, since both blocks are individually learnable to criterion.
- **Decision enabled** — this motivates testing retention-preserving training such
  as replay or interleaving before attempting a larger sequential curriculum. It
  does not show that replay or interleaving will work; that is untested.
- **Caveat** — the sequential run deliberately starts a fresh AdamW over the same
  N0 scope (the source checkpoint stores no optimizer state), which is a recorded
  confound for the acquisition slowdown, though not for the forgetting.

## Fig backup — Frozen model starting point
`fig_backup_frozen_starting_point.png` / `.pdf`

- **Question** — what did the frozen model already do before any task training?
- **Population** — full lexicon, mean of the 4 canonical seeds.
- **Metric** — comprehension top-1 (%), naming exact-match (%).
- **Main interpretation** — frozen comprehension 2.55%, naming from true GloVe
  exactly 0.00%, but naming from the model's own internal `s_hat` 98.73%. The
  decoder is already highly functional from its learned internal code; raw GloVe
  is simply not yet an interchangeable interface.
- **Decision enabled** — the two tasks genuinely required explicit training; this
  is the baseline against which the main figures are read. Supporting context,
  not part of the main package.
