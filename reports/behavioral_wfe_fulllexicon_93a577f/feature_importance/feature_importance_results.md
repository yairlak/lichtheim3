# Sprint 5 — adapted feature importance (A15): results

Cohort `fulllexicon_cohort_93a577f`, training commit `93a577f`, seeds 19, 20,
21, 22. Every number comes from the canonical seed × item × route table.
**No checkpoint was loaded and no model inference was run**; the only models
fitted are the Ridge regressions described in the frozen specification.

Specification frozen before any model was fitted:
`feature_importance_analysis_spec.md`.

---

## 1. Scope

Three adapted analyses on `LICHTHEIM_CLEAN`:

- **A** clean joint main-effects model — **primary**;
- **B** clean interaction model with five predeclared two-way blocks —
  secondary;
- **C** route-specific models, one per route — secondary.

The faithful Dager analysis (A11) is a **separate** analysis; it was not
recomputed, replaced or pooled. See `faithful_vs_adapted.md`. No SSP, no causal
claim, no architectural recommendation.

---

## 2. Identifiability constraints

On the clean set **lexicality and training exposure are perfectly confounded**:
every Real item is `TRAINED_REAL_EXACT` (671) and every Pseudo item is
`NOVEL_PSEUDOWORD` (391); the 2 × 2 cross-tabulation has exactly two non-empty
cells. Consequently:

- the two never enter the same model;
- **no claim is made that these models separate lexicality from exposure**;
- the factor is reported throughout as the **lexicality/exposure contrast**.

Continuous Zipf frequency is **undefined for pseudowords**, is never imputed as
zero, and is excluded from every all-item clean model. The trained-real
frequency analysis is Sprint 3 (A14).

Morphology (simple/complex) and phoneme length (3, 4, 5, 7, 8, 9) are valid
clean-set factors; length 6 is absent from the WFE by construction.

## 4. Adapted main-effects model performance

Split: **850 train / 212 test items**, grouped by `item_id`, `random_state = 42`,
**identical across all four seeds and all three models**. 2,550 train rows and
636 test rows per seed (212 items × 3 routes).

| seed | train R² | **test R²** | test MAE | test rows > 0 | status |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 19 | 0.1160 | **0.1201** | 0.2044 | 23 | `OK` |
| 20 | 0.0969 | **0.0941** | 0.1780 | 21 | `OK` |
| 21 | 0.1077 | **0.0760** | 0.1823 | 19 | `OK` |
| 22 | 0.1006 | **0.0907** | 0.1692 | 18 | `OK` |

Held-out R² is positive in all four seeds (mean 0.0952, range 0.0760–0.1201).
Train and test R² are close, so the main-effects model is not overfitting; it
simply explains a modest share of a highly zero-inflated outcome — only 18–23 of
636 held-out rows are non-zero.

## 5. Adapted grouped factor importance

Primary measure: **drop in held-out R²** when a raw factor is permuted across
held-out items (route within item), with all derived columns rebuilt; 100
repeats, `random_state = 42`. Mean over seeds [range], with the mean within-seed
permutation SD:

| factor | **R² drop** | range over seeds | within-seed SD | MAE increase |
|---|---:|---|---:|---:|
| **lexicality/exposure** | **0.0777** | [0.0704, 0.0832] | 0.015 | 0.0101 |
| **route** | **0.0842** | [0.0734, 0.1051] | 0.015 | 0.0078 |
| **length** | **0.0473** | [0.0312, 0.0625] | 0.013 | 0.0069 |
| morphology | **0.0009** | [−0.0006, 0.0014] | 0.001 | 0.0006 |

Route and lexicality/exposure are the two leading factors and are close enough
that their order swaps between seeds. Length is a clear third. **Morphology is
effectively zero** — its mean R² drop is two orders of magnitude below the
leaders, is negative in seed 20, and is smaller than its own within-seed
permutation SD in three of four seeds.

Grouped importance is **unsigned**: no single sign is attached to route or to
any other multilevel factor.

Figure: `clean_joint/figures/clean_adapted_factor_importance.*`, with within-seed
permutation SD drawn as a whisker and between-seed variation as the spread
between seed markers, so the two are not confused.

## 6. Coefficients and sign interpretation

Reported **separately** from importance, relative to the frozen reference levels
`route = WM`, `lexicality = Pseudoword`, `morphology = Complex`
(`clean_joint/tables/clean_main_model_coefficients.tsv`):

| term | seed 19 | seed 20 | seed 21 | seed 22 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| length (standardized) | +0.0826 | +0.0605 | +0.0806 | +0.0627 |
| lexicality: Real − Pseudo | −0.1787 | −0.1676 | −0.2080 | −0.1832 |
| route: **LTM − WM** | +0.2265 | +0.1925 | +0.1984 | +0.2078 |
| route: **FULL − WM** | +0.0009 | −0.0026 | −0.0155 | −0.0026 |
| morphology: Simple − Complex | +0.0262 | −0.0046 | +0.0253 | +0.0076 |

Read as: longer items have more edit operations (positive in all four seeds);

and WM are indistinguishable (|coefficient| ≤ 0.016, sign not consistent).
Morphology changes sign across seeds.

Route is reported as **two numbers**, LTM − WM and FULL − WM, and is never
collapsed into one signed value.

## 7. Interaction-model utility

The interaction model adds held-out information in **all four seeds**
(`clean_interactions/tables/interaction_model_incremental_utility.tsv`):

| seed | main R² | interaction R² | **Δ R²** | main MAE | interaction MAE | **Δ MAE** |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 19 | 0.1201 | 0.2644 | **+0.1444** | 0.2044 | 0.1868 | **+0.0177** |
| 20 | 0.0941 | 0.1865 | **+0.0924** | 0.1780 | 0.1641 | **+0.0139** |
| 21 | 0.0760 | 0.1731 | **+0.0971** | 0.1823 | 0.1612 | **+0.0210** |
| 22 | 0.0907 | 0.2044 | **+0.1137** | 0.1692 | 0.1564 | **+0.0128** |

Both criteria improve in every seed, so the figure condition (utility in at
least two seeds) was met and
`clean_interactions/figures/interaction_block_utility.*` was produced.

Drop-block utility — held-out R² lost when one predeclared block is removed and
the model refitted (mean over seeds [range]):

| block | **R² utility** | MAE utility |
|---|---|---|
| **route × lexicality** | **+0.0640** [0.0353, 0.0766] | +0.0151 |
| **lexicality × length** | **+0.0289** [0.0162, 0.0418] | −0.0160 |
| **route × length** | **+0.0287** [0.0149, 0.0360] | −0.0158 |
| route × morphology | +0.0003 [−0.0009, 0.0023] | −0.0003 |
| morphology × length | −0.0005 [−0.0017, 0.0003] | −0.0001 |

`route × lexicality` is the only block positive on **both** criteria in every
seed. `lexicality × length` and `route × length` help R² consistently but
slightly **hurt** MAE — an honest divergence between the two scores that is
reported rather than resolved; on the frozen policy, R² is primary and MAE is
the stability check, so these two blocks are `CONSISTENT_BUT_SMALL` rather than
robust. The two morphology blocks are indistinguishable from zero.

Blocks were declared before fitting; there was no post-hoc selection, and no
block is called important on the strength of a coefficient.

## 10. Exact-zero sensitivity

Seeds 19, 20 and 22 are reported in a separate `exact_zero_seeds_mean` column in
every summary table and **never replace** the four-seed primary result. The two
agree closely: for example the primary R² drop for lexicality/exposure is 0.0777

versus 0.0878. The factor ordering is unchanged. No conclusion depends on which
subset is used.

## 11. R² versus MAE sensitivity

Both scores are reported for every model and every factor because the outcome is
zero-heavy (18–23 non-zero rows out of 636).

- **Factor importance**: the two scores agree on the extremes and disagree in
  the middle. Under **MAE**, lexicality/exposure is **first in all four seeds**
  and morphology last in all four, but route falls to second (seeds 20, 22) or
  third (seeds 19, 21), exchanging places with length. Under **R²**, route is
  first in three seeds. So the two scores agree that lexicality/exposure and
  route are the leading factors and morphology is negligible, but **they do not
  agree on which single factor leads**, which is the same unresolved
  route-versus-lexicality separation reported in §9.
- **Interaction blocks**: the two **disagree** for `lexicality × length` and
  `route × length`, which improve R² but slightly worsen MAE. This is reported,
  not reconciled, and those blocks are classified conservatively.
- **Route-specific FULL and WM**: R² is non-estimable or unstable while MAE
  remains finite and tiny. MAE is the more interpretable sensitivity there, and
  a small MAE increase on a near-all-zero outcome is not evidence of a factor
  effect.

## 12. Ceiling and sparse-error limitations

- **Ceiling.** FULL and WM produce almost no held-out errors on the clean set
  (0–2 non-zero rows out of 212 per seed). Their route-specific models are
  `INSUFFICIENT_ERRORS` or `NON_ESTIMABLE`; the models are kept and labelled.
- **Zero inflation.** Even the joint model has only 18–23 non-zero held-out
  rows, so held-out R² is modest by construction and MAE is reported alongside.
- **Confounding.** Lexicality and exposure cannot be separated on this set, by
  construction. Any statement about "lexicality" here is a statement about the
  lexicality/exposure contrast.
- **Frequency omitted.** Zipf is undefined for pseudowords, so no all-item clean
  model can include it; its absence is a scope limit, not a finding.
- **Split size.** 212 held-out items is small; importance values carry
  meaningful within-seed permutation spread, which the figure shows explicitly.
- **Route is a design factor**, not a stimulus property: its importance says how
  much knowing the route helps predict errors, nothing about mechanism.

---

## 13. Robust findings

- **Morphology carries essentially no predictive weight** in the adapted clean
  models: last-ranked in all four seeds, mean R² drop 0.0009, negative in one
  seed, and its two interaction blocks are indistinguishable from zero. This
  agrees with the Sprint-2 conclusion that no morphology contrast was robust.
  **This is a statement about predictive contribution to these Ridge models on
  this outcome, not a proof that morphology is absent from the model's internal
  representations.** A factor can be represented and still add nothing to a
  linear prediction of edit distance on a zero-heavy held-out set.
- **Length ranks third in all four seeds** with a positive standardized
  coefficient in all four (`STABLE_RANKING`).
- **Route and lexicality/exposure are the two leading factors** in all four
  seeds, jointly and by a wide margin over length. **Their ordering relative to
  each other is not robust and is not claimed**: their mean R² drops differ by
  only ≈ 0.0064, which is smaller than the within-seed permutation SD of
  ≈ 0.015; they exchange ranks in seed 21; and under MAE lexicality/exposure
  ranks first in every seed. Only their joint leadership over length and
  morphology is asserted.
- **Only the LTM route is estimable** for route-specific feature importance;
  within it, lexicality/exposure > length > morphology in all four seeds.
- **The interaction model beats the main-effects model on held-out data in all
  four seeds**, on both R² and MAE.
- **`route × lexicality` is the strongest interaction block**, positive on both
  criteria in every seed.

## 14. Stable descriptive findings

- LTM − WM is strongly positive and FULL − WM is indistinguishable from zero in
  all four seeds — consistent with, and not independent of, the Sprint-1 route
  results.
- Exact-zero sensitivity leaves every ordering unchanged.
- `lexicality × length` and `route × length` are `CONSISTENT_BUT_SMALL`:
  consistently positive on R², slightly negative on MAE.

## 15. Unstable or non-estimable findings

- **`UNSTABLE_ACROSS_SEEDS` / unresolved**: the route versus lexicality/exposure
  ordering — the gap is smaller than the within-seed permutation SD.
- **`NON_ESTIMABLE`**: FULL seeds 20 and 22, WM seed 22 — held-out outcome has
  no variance, so R² is undefined.
- **`INSUFFICIENT_ERRORS`**: FULL seeds 19 and 21, WM seeds 19, 20 and 21.
- **`NEGATIVE_TEST_R2`**: WM seed 21 (−0.0257), retained and flagged.
- **`CEILING_LIMITED`**: all FULL and WM route-specific importance.
- Morphology's sign is not consistent across seeds in any model.

**An unstable ranking is not evidence that a factor is unrepresented**, and a
ceiling-limited route is not a route without structure.

## 16. Faithful-versus-adapted distinction

Kept entirely separate; see `faithful_vs_adapted.md`. A11 and A15 differ in item
population, route scope, split unit and permutation semantics, and their values
are never pooled, averaged, differenced or drawn on one axis. The two disagree
qualitatively about the length-versus-lexicality ordering, which the differences
above are sufficient to explain and which validates neither analysis against the
other.
